# Optical S/PDIF Loopback Measurements

These measurements were made on one RASPIAUDIO lab Raspberry Pi 5 setup.

Test chain:

```text
RASPISPDIF ALSA playback
    -> Raspberry Pi 5 GPIO12 S/PDIF
    -> TOSLINK transmitter
    -> optical cable
    -> TOSLINK-to-analog converter
    -> RASPIAUDIO 8xIN+8xOUT ADC input
```

This is a complete functional loopback measurement. It validates that the
generated S/PDIF stream carries continuous audio through a real optical receiver
path. It does not isolate the Raspberry Pi S/PDIF generator from the external
TOSLINK converter or the ADC.

## Result Snapshot

Command:

```bash
cd ~/CamillaDSP/prototypes/pi5_spdif_gpio
ADC_DEVICE=hw:CARD=sndrpihifiberry,DEV=0 ADC_CHANNELS=8 \
  ./scripts/measure_optical_loopback_adc.py
```

Observed result on the lab setup:

| Item | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Active ADC channels | `IN0`, `IN1` |
| Channel mapping | `IN0 = Left`, `IN1 = Right` |
| Complete loopback latency | about `3 ms` to `4 ms` |
| Dropout blocks during 10 s 1 kHz tone | `0` |
| 1 kHz level stability | about `0.003 dB` block drift |
| Captured 1 kHz level | about `-25 dBFS` |
| Left/right crosstalk | about `-69 dB` |
| Pre-play ADC noise floor | about `-80 dBFS` |
| Rough harmonic THD of complete chain | around `-75 dB` |
| Frequency response, 30 Hz to 15 kHz | within about `2 dB` |
| Frequency response at 20 Hz | about `-3.5 dB` |
| Frequency response at 20 kHz | about `-0.25 dB` |

## What These Numbers Mean

The strongest result is continuity: the optical stream played for the test
window with no 50 ms mute blocks. That is the important validation for the Pi 5
RP1 PIO + DMA S/PDIF driver.

The loopback latency number is useful for system integration, but it is the
whole test chain latency. It includes the TOSLINK transmitter, external optical
DAC/converter, analog output stage, and ADC capture path.

The channel mapping and crosstalk numbers are also useful practical checks. They
prove that left/right audio survives the S/PDIF path and arrives on the expected
ADC channels with good separation for this lab chain.

The frequency response is mostly a sanity check of the optical converter plus
ADC path. The low-frequency rolloff is likely dominated by the inexpensive
TOSLINK-to-analog converter, not by S/PDIF itself.

## What Is Safe To Claim

Good public wording:

```text
The Raspberry Pi 5 S/PDIF prototype was validated with a real optical loopback:
GPIO12 -> TOSLINK transmitter -> optical receiver/DAC -> RASPIAUDIO ADC.
In this lab setup it produced stable 48 kHz stereo playback, correct left/right
mapping, about 3 ms to 4 ms complete loopback latency, and no 50 ms dropout blocks
during the 10 second 1 kHz continuity test.
```

Also reasonable:

```text
The loopback measurement includes the external optical converter and ADC. It is
a functional validation, not a standalone jitter, SNR, or THD+N specification.
```

## What Not To Claim Yet

Do not publish these as final product specifications from this loopback alone:

- S/PDIF jitter.
- S/PDIF eye pattern or optical compliance.
- Product SNR.
- Product THD+N.
- Universal receiver compatibility.

Those need either a proper S/PDIF analyzer, a known high-quality optical
receiver/DAC, or a calibrated audio analyzer setup.

## How Good Is It?

For a prototype generated by Raspberry Pi 5 RP1 PIO + DMA, the result is good.
The important engineering risks were audible gaps, unstable receiver lock,
wrong speed, left/right mapping mistakes, and DMA underruns. This loopback test
did not show those problems.

For audiophile performance claims, the current bench is not enough. The measured
`-75 dB` rough THD and low-frequency rolloff are more likely limitations of the
cheap optical-to-analog converter and the ADC loopback than of the digital
S/PDIF stream. Use these figures as lab validation, not as the ceiling of the
final product.

The next useful proof would be a long-duration receiver lock test and a capture
with a better known receiver or analyzer.
